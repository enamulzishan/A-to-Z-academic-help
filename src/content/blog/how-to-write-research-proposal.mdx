## Understanding the Purpose

A research proposal is a document that outlines your proposed study. Its primary purpose is to convince an academic committee that your research is **worth doing**, and that you are **capable of doing it**.

## The Key Sections

### The Introduction and Rationale
Start strong. Why does this research matter? Set the context and explicitly state the problem you are addressing.

### The Literature Review
Demonstrate your knowledge of the field. Show how your research builds upon existing work rather than just repeating it.

### Research Questions
These should be clear, focused, and measurable. Avoid vague or overly broad questions.

### Methodology
Explain *how* you will answer your research questions. Will you use qualitative interviews? Quantitative surveys? A mixed-methods approach? Justify your choice.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- **Being overly ambitious**: A PhD is not meant to solve all the world's problems. It is a specific, narrow contribution to knowledge.
- **Ignoring feasibility**: Don't propose a methodology that requires a £50,000 budget if you have no funding.
